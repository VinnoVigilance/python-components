## 0. One-paragraph summary

The encoder step (notebook 02) is finished: no encoder beats char TF-IDF, and whole-record models fail on new lists
mainly through **precision**. We therefore built and tested the agreed **evidence-unit design**: rules → *highlighter*
(cut records into pieces, flag pieces with trigger words; notebook 03) → *checker* (a local LLM reads each flagged piece
with the label's taxonomy definition and answers yes/no; notebook 04). On the 15 labels in scope, against a reviewed
reference, the checker reaches **precision 0.82 / recall 0.89** (best whole-record model: 0.54 / 0.66), **0.92 precision
on wanted lists** but **0.71 on sanctions lists**. The user's target is **precision ≥ 90%**. The checker needs a GPU
(≈ 9 min per 1,000 records on the RTX 4070; ≈ 25 h per 1,000 on CPU), and the company server has **no GPU**, so the
proposed production path is a **small "student" model distilled from the LLM**, run on CPU. Decisions and open
questions are in section 9.

## 5. Notebook 04 — the checker

Code: `scripts/checker.py`; notebook `notebooks/04_checker.ipynb` + `_explained.md`.

* **Model:** `Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507` (Apache-2.0), 4-bit (bitsandbytes NF4), RTX 4070, **fully local**.
* **One question per flagged (piece, label):** label definition / coverage / boundary (taxonomy column C), sample
  indicators (column E, v2), the field name, the piece, the piece before and after it (same record), and
  "does this support assigning the label to the listed person or entity?".
* **Score:** P(yes) of the first answer token, one model pass (`logits_to_keep=1` — without it GPU memory overflowed and
  the run crawled). Record gets a label if any flagged piece scores ≥ 0.5. **No training on our labels**, so every list
  is "new" to it. A per-label tuned cut-off was no better than 0.5.
* **Question v2** (`PROMPT_VERSION = "v2"`, caches `cache/checker_pyes_{v1,v2}.csv`): + indicators; "victims, missing
  persons, officials fighting the conduct, the list's publishing authority, laws and programme names do not count";
  "one event can carry several labels"; PEP note "leaders of militias / non-state armed groups hold no public function".
  Fixed: robbery → Theft (was missed), militia leader → PEP (was wrongly yes). Not fixed: "Homicide Victim" → Violent.

| 15 labels, original reference | Precision | Recall | F1 | Wrong labels added |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| best whole-record model (nb 02, unseen lists) | 0.518 | 0.697 | 0.594 | 395 |
| highlighter only | 0.476 | 0.948 | 0.634 | 636 |
| checker v1 | 0.706 | 0.867 | 0.779 | 220 |
| **checker v2** | **0.733** | **0.875** | **0.798** | **195** |

* **Written reasons are not trustworthy** with this 4B model (invented "false promises of high returns"; a written
  "yes" after a scored "no"). Decision = score; evidence shown to a reviewer = the flagged sentence itself.
* **Cost:** 0.15 s per question (v2) → ≈ 9.4 min per 1,000 records on the RTX 4070; 300k records ≈ 47 h (≈ 27 h with a
  cache of identical pieces). **On the laptop CPU: ≈ 24 s per question ≈ 25 h per 1,000 records — unusable.**

## 7. Answers to the user's questions this session

**Is the checker just keyword matching?** No. The highlighter matches words (and must not miss anything); the checker
reads meaning. "Head of Department for **Combating** Corruption" → no; "executive role in a **bribing** scheme" → yes;
"The Global Anti-Corruption Sanctions Regulations" → no (programme name).

**Must the model run on every record?** No: records with no flagged piece (≈ 27%) need no call; only flagged pieces
(≈ 2.9 per record) are sent; identical pieces are answered once (cache); in daily use only new / changed records.

**How can the teacher's 100,000 answers be trusted?** They cannot be blindly. Plan:
1. The teacher's quality is measured on the 900 (precision 0.82 reviewed) and on the **fresh 300** before use.
2. **Two-teacher agreement:** run both Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B; train the student mainly on pieces where they agree;
   disagreements go to review / are left out.
3. Rule fixes (section 8 A) are applied before generating answers.
4. A human spot-check of ~200 random teacher answers, with a confidence interval, before training.
5. The student is finally judged on human-reviewed / reviewed labels, never on the teacher's answers.

**New lists with words the highlighter does not know?** Real risk. v1 words come from the taxonomy; v2 words are
general conduct / title / ES-FR-DE-PT words written by Claude after reading samples of this data (so slightly
optimistic); no list names or list-specific rules. Gaps: other languages (Russian, Arabic, Persian…) and new legal
wording. Proposed **onboarding check for every new list**: take ~50 of its records, let the teacher read **all** pieces
(not only flagged ones) for all 15 labels, compare with the highlighter, add missing words / translations, re-test.
Records with long text but zero flags are also sampled periodically.

### How the student works (for discussion)

```
once, on the laptop GPU:   records ─► highlighter ─► teacher(s) answer each (piece, label) ─► student is fine-tuned to copy
every day, on the server:  new record ─► lookup ─► highlighter ─► student (CPU) ─► labels + evidence sentence
                                                                      └─ uncertain ─► review queue
```

* Not pre-trained from scratch: we start from a model that already knows language and **fine-tune** it.
* It sees short pieces + the label definition, never whole records or list names, so it should not learn list styles
  (the reason notebook 02's whole-record fine-tuning failed on new lists). This must be **proven** on unseen-list folds
  and the fresh 300.
* Retrain only when the taxonomy / definitions change or monitoring shows quality dropping — not per new list.
* Expected weaker than the teacher; the review queue protects precision at the cost of manual work.
